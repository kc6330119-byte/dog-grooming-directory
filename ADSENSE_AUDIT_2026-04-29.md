# DGL AdSense Pre-Submission Audit — 2026-04-29

**Auditor:** Claude (Opus 4.7, 1M context)
**Scope:** Audit Dog Groomer Locator against the failure modes that triggered Google AdSense's "Low Value Content" rejection of Holistic Vet Directory.
**Method:** Read templates and `build.py`; sampled production pages over HTTPS (5 groomer + 30 groomer-stats + 10 city + 5 state); parsed sitemap (6,150 URLs).
**Verdict:** DGL is **closer to AdSense-ready than HVD was**, but ships with **two critical structural risks** (city doorway pages, indexed-page ratio) and **one critical data-quality risk** (junk descriptions passing the thin-listing filter). All three are fixable before resubmission.

---

## A. Indexed-page ratio — **CRITICAL**

**Finding (sitemap parsed at https://doggroomerlocator.com/sitemap.xml):**

| Page type      | URLs  | %     |
| -------------- | ----- | ----- |
| Groomer detail | 5,299 | 86.2% |
| City           | 749   | 12.2% |
| State          | 50    | 0.8%  |
| Category       | 8     | 0.1%  |
| Blog posts     | 35    | 0.6%  |
| Static editorial (about, contact, privacy, terms, disclaimer, editorial-standards, dog-grooming-guide, blog index) | 8 | 0.1% |
| **Total**      | **6,150** | |

- **Directory pages: 6,106 (99.3%)**
- **Editorial content: 43 (0.7%)** — 35 blog posts + 8 static
- **Worse than HVD's 98% / 1% ratio.** This is the worst signal in the audit.

**Risk:** Critical. AdSense reviewers comparing a 6,150-page site where 99% are listings and <1% are articles will flag it under "Low Value Content / Doorway pages" almost immediately. The directory is the product, but to pass AdSense the editorial-to-directory ratio has to look more like a content site.

**Recommended fix:** Either (a) raise the editorial floor — get to ~150–200 published blog posts so editorial is ≥3% of indexed pages — or (b) lower the directory ceiling by tightening the noindex threshold (see F) so fewer thin groomer/city pages enter the index. The fastest path is (b) combined with an aggressive blog cadence; (a) alone takes months to land.

## B. Templated FAQ blocks on groomer detail pages — **NONE**

**Finding:**
- Searched `templates/groomer.html` (365 lines): no FAQ generator, no Question/Answer schema, no "What services does X offer?" block.
- Sampled 5 random rendered pages from production (`sherrie-s-pet-grooming-bellevue`, `the-pet-palace-webster`, `on-the-ball-prairie-village`, `pawfect-pet-saloon-essie`, `under-one-ruff-cleburne`). Grepped for "FAQ", "Frequently asked", "What services", "Services are tailored": **0 hits across all 5.**
- No templated wrapper sentence equivalent to HVD's "What holistic services does X offer? X offers Y. Services are tailored…"

**Risk:** None. This is the single biggest structural difference vs. HVD, and DGL has it right.

**Recommended fix:** None. **Do not add an FAQ block** during any future "improve detail pages" work — that would inherit the HVD problem.

## C. Templated service / category descriptions on detail pages — **LOW–MEDIUM**

**Finding:**
- 8 service category pages (`/category/full-service`, `/category/mobile-grooming`, etc.) each have unique 70–110 word `intro` paragraphs (`config.py:156–181`).
- **These intros stay on the category landing page only.** They are NOT injected into individual groomer detail pages. Verified by reading `templates/groomer.html` end-to-end — services are rendered as tag pills (`{{ service }}` inside a styled span), no editorial paragraph per service. No equivalent to HVD's verbatim "About Acupuncture" block appearing on 1,000+ pages.
- However, the **groomer.description** field (sourced from Airtable, displayed verbatim on detail pages) shows mild templating in the Airtable-supplied data itself: across 30 random production pages, "comprehensive grooming services" appeared on 8 (27%), "we provide" on 2, "committed to keeping your pet" on 1. This is mild signal — not a fixed wrapper — but Google's classifier picks up on phrase repetition at scale.

**Risk:** Low–Medium. The architecture is right; the data is somewhat templated but not at HVD's "100% identical wrapper" severity.

**Recommended fix:** Pass through the existing groomer descriptions for the worst-offender phrases ("comprehensive grooming services" first) and rewrite or strip them in Airtable. Don't change the rendering — change the source data. Optionally add a one-time content review pass on the 5,316 listings to flag descriptions starting with the same N-grams.

## D. City pages — **CRITICAL (this is the doorway pattern)**

**Finding:**
- 749 city pages exist (sitemap-confirmed).
- **All 10 sampled city pages have the IDENTICAL templated wrapper.** Word counts ranged 48–57. The wrapper is generated by `build.py:418–434` (`generate_city_intro`):

  > "{City}, {State} has N professional dog grooming {business/businesses} listed in our directory, offering {types} services. Whether your dog needs a routine bath and trim, breed-specific styling, or specialized care for sensitive skin, {City}'s grooming professionals are ready to help keep your pet looking and feeling their best."

  Only `{City}`, `{State}`, `N`, and `{types}` vary. The 30+ word tail is verbatim across all 749 city pages.
- **Listings on the city page are a strict subset of the parent state page** (no unique listings, just filtered duplicates).
- **No unique editorial content** beyond the templated intro on any of the 10 sampled pages.
- **`MIN_LISTINGS_FOR_INDEX = 3` (config.py:31) only protects the worst case.** Cities with 2 listings get noindex (verified: `city_3.html` Sun City West, `city_4.html` East New Market, `city_5.html` Bigfork — all show `<meta name="robots" content="noindex, follow">`). But cities with 3+ listings — including the indistinguishable 50-word templated intro — are still in the index.
- **Earlier Opus 4.7 analysis cited "0.4% of city pages getting traffic"** — confirmed plausible: with 749 indexed templated city pages and no unique content, most never rank.

**Risk:** Critical. This is verbatim the HVD doorway pattern AdSense's policy page calls out, and it's at scale: 749 indexed pages all sharing one 30-word verbatim sentence is exactly the signal Google's classifier is built to detect.

**Recommended fix:** Two-stage. Short term: raise the noindex threshold to require `>= 5` listings AND any unique editorial content beyond the boilerplate (this likely drops indexed city pages from 749 to ~150–200). Long term: either invest in genuine per-city editorial (real local color: which neighborhoods have salons, what the local market rates are) or remove the city tier entirely and let users land on state pages with city filters. Don't ship templated city intros — they are a liability, not an asset.

## E. Groomer detail page uniqueness — **HIGH**

**Finding:**
- Sampled 30 random production pages. Total page word count: 448–498 words.
- **Description block word count: 16–79 words, median 53.** None of the 30 pages had ≥100 words of unique description.
- Of 30 pages: 2 had <20 words, 10 had 20–50, 18 had 50–100, **0 had 100+**.
- Subtracting header/nav/footer/sidebar/related-listings boilerplate (~390 words/page on this template), **unique-content ratio is ~10–15%** — worse than HVD's measured ~19% (80/420).
- Concrete junk-data example: `/groomer/under-one-ruff-cleburne` — its description is **scraped tattoo-studio metadata in German**: `"@bibo_stp | TATTOO PIERCING br Talstrasse 25 br Reeperbahn br br FFNUNGSZEITEN br Mo So 13 21 Uhr. Check out my links to (WhatsApp !, Jetzt Anrufen !, Instagram)."` That page is **currently indexed as `index, follow`**, because the description is >100 chars and other data fields are populated.

**Risk:** High. Two distinct problems: (1) descriptions are short across the board, (2) some descriptions are non-grooming spam/scrape that would embarrass an AdSense reviewer who saw it.

**Recommended fix:** Two changes. (1) Raise `MIN_DESCRIPTION_LENGTH` from 100 → 250 and require it to be the dominant content gate, not one signal of four (see F). (2) Add a content-quality filter: reject descriptions containing obvious off-topic markers (URLs in body text, `@username` mentions, non-grooming category words like "tattoo", "piercing", non-English unless the listing's state is bilingual). Even simple heuristics would have caught the Under One Ruff case.

## F. Quality threshold review — **CRITICAL**

**Finding:**
- DGL's equivalent of HVD's `MIN_VET_QUALITY` is **`is_thin_listing()` at `build.py:402–415`**:
  ```
  desc_ok    = len(desc) >= MIN_DESCRIPTION_LENGTH (100 chars)
  hours_ok   = bool(hours)
  services_ok = isinstance(services, list) and len(services) > 0
  phone_ok   = bool(phone)
  noindex if (desc_ok + hours_ok + services_ok + phone_ok) <= 1
  ```
- **Measures DATA COMPLETENESS, not CONTENT UNIQUENESS or QUALITY** — exactly the gap the prompt flagged on HVD.
- A page passes (gets indexed) if **2 of 4** signals are present. Real-world consequence: Under One Ruff (Cleburne) has corrupt scraped tattoo-studio German text as its description but is **currently indexed** because that text is >100 chars (desc_ok), it has a phone, and one more signal — that's enough.
- The threshold **never inspects WHAT the description says**, only whether one exists.

**Risk:** Critical. This is the single most leveraged fix in the audit: tightening this one function changes the indexed page set for the entire site.

**Recommended fix:** Re-architect `is_thin_listing` as a **content-quality gate**, not a data-completeness gate. Treat description length + uniqueness as the primary gate (e.g., require ≥250 chars AND must not match a known-bad pattern AND must mention "groom"/"dog"/"pet"/"salon" or related terms). Demote hours/phone/services to secondary signals that *boost* a borderline page rather than substitute for content. Re-run the build and expect 5,299 indexed groomer pages to drop substantially — that's the goal, not a regression.

## G. State page editorial depth — **HIGH**

**Finding:**
- 50 unique state descriptions in `config.py:52–153`.
- Sampled 5 (Alabama, Alaska, Arizona, Arkansas, California): word counts **40, 45, 44, 40, 57**.
- **All five are below the 200-word "shallow" threshold** the prompt set. Median appears ~50 words.
- Each description IS unique per state (no template wrapper) — that's better than HVD's state pages — but they are too short to count as substantive editorial.

**Risk:** High. Not as severe as the city doorway pattern (these are unique), but with 50 state pages averaging ~50 words of editorial each, state pages don't carry editorial weight. They function as listing index pages, not destination pages.

**Recommended fix:** Expand each state description to 200–300 words. Add genuine local context: regional grooming price ranges, which cities in the state have the strongest grooming market, climate-driven coat-care notes specific to that state. The `config.py` data structure is already there; this is a content task, not an engineering one. Lower priority than D and F.

## H. Schema-HTML parity — **LOW (refutes earlier 37.5% finding)**

**Finding (verified on `/groomer/pawfect-pet-saloon-essie`):**
- 3 JSON-LD scripts on the page: `WebSite` (from base), `BreadcrumbList`, `LocalBusiness`.
- `LocalBusiness` declares: `name, description, address, telephone, url, hasMap, openingHours, priceRange` (8 fields; aggregateRating + image only included when source data has them).
- Visible HTML on the same page contains: name (h1) ✓, description (About This Groomer) ✓, address (in details table) ✓, phone (sidebar) ✓, website (button) ✓, Google Maps link ✓, hours (in details + sidebar) ✓, price-range (badge + details) ✓, rating stars + count ✓.
- **8/8 fields declared have matching visible HTML.** The earlier Opus 4.7 "37.5%" claim does not match the current code; either the templates were changed since Apr 17 (note: groomer.html was last modified Apr 12 per `ls`, so likely the prior measurement was scoped differently — perhaps to listing cards rather than detail pages, or to a different page type).
- One minor schema lint: `openingHours` is emitted as the raw Airtable string (e.g. "Mon-Fri: 7am - 7pm, Sat: 8am - 5pm"), which technically isn't the schema.org `openingHours` format (`Mo-Fr 07:00-19:00`). This is a validity issue, not a parity issue.

**Risk:** Low. Schema-HTML parity on detail pages is high. The `openingHours` format is a separate validity nit.

**Recommended fix:** Optional. Normalize `openingHours` to schema.org canonical format if you want clean Rich Results Test output. Not an AdSense blocker.

## I. Sitemap freshness — **MEDIUM**

**Finding:**
- `build.py:671–736` correctly uses **per-page lastmod**, not build-time. State page lastmod = max(`last_modified`) of its groomers; city pages do the same; groomer pages use their own `last_modified`; posts use their `modified_date` (capped at today, fix from earlier this month).
- However, the **data** tells a different story. Of 6,150 sitemap entries:
  - **5,582 (90.8%) share lastmod `2026-03-31`**
  - 517 (8.4%) share `2026-04-01`
  - 51 entries spread across 21 other dates
- Only 23 unique lastmod dates total across 6,150 URLs.
- **Root cause: the underlying Airtable `Last Modified` field for ~91% of groomer records is `2026-03-31`** (likely the bulk import date). The sitemap code is correct; the data isn't moving.
- Earlier Opus 4.7's "91% same lastmod" finding stands.

**Risk:** Medium. Google reads the sitemap as "this site does not change," which suppresses recrawl frequency. Not a direct AdSense rejection trigger like D/E/F, but it compounds them — stale sitemap means slower propagation of any fixes you ship.

**Recommended fix:** Touch the `Last Modified` field in Airtable on a rolling cadence as you do listing maintenance. Or, in `build.py`, derive lastmod from a per-listing content hash + a stored "last seen changed" date so meaningful edits move the date and noise edits don't. Cheap interim option: when you redeploy after fixing D/E/F, set lastmod to the build date for any listing whose noindex status changed — that gives Google a clean recrawl signal.

**DGL is structurally healthier than HVD on detail pages (B, C, H)** — no FAQ block, no per-specialty editorial repeated 1,000× — but it **inherits HVD's exact city-doorway pattern (D)**, has a **slightly worse indexed-page ratio (A)**, has **the same data-completeness-not-content-quality threshold gap (F)**, and ships with at least one **visibly broken listing (Under One Ruff)** that AdSense reviewers might happen across.

**Two-line bottom call:** Don't submit yet. Fix D + F + the description-quality gate from E first (a 1–2 day engineering effort once the design is settled), then talk about A.
